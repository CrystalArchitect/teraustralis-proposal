0. Status

This brief records public layers of Dharawal and Sydney Basin knowledge that named holders and institutions have chosen to share for teaching and site care. It is not a closed cosmology. It is not a TerAustralis story. It does not complete, fuse, or extend Songlines. Starlines, CrystalCore, Catch, Gate, and CICH remain other objects.

Restricted, gendered, or community-only knowledge is out of scope. We do not ask for it. We do not invent it. This page is noindex. It is not a door on www.teraustralis.com.au.

1. Country

Country is not a backdrop. In the public Dharawal accounts used here, sky, land, sea, animals, plants, people, and the spirits of the dead form one living field. The sitting is written on Dharawal Country (Sydney south-west sandstone, including the Campbelltown–Bradbury–Appin corridor). That is geography and responsibility. It is not a selected berth.

If a place is named for industrial work, Traditional Owners first. Document 16 §5 and Document 06 hold the legal floor. This brief does not weaken it.

3. Sydney Basin engravings (public facts)

The Basin holds one of the densest concentrations of Aboriginal petroglyphs in Australia — on the order of two thousand recorded open engraving sites plus shelter pigment sites. Most locations remain unpublished to protect them and because they are sacred. This sitting will not list them.

Style. Simple Figurative: outline figures, pecked then abraded. Public motifs include marine animals, land animals, human figures and footprints (mundoes), objects, and larger ancestral beings.

Date. Grooves are not directly radiocarbon-dated. The style is generally associated with the last several thousand years. Occupation of the region is much older. Treat dates as provisional.

Living status. Not prehistoric relics. Cultural landscape for Dharawal, Gai-mariagal, Dharug and neighbouring peoples. Knowledge holders are the authority where they choose to speak.

Culture-hero types (research labels, not identifications on this page): Baiame-type; Daramulan-type. We do not pin a name to an unpublished platform.

4. One public site: Jibbon / Djeeban

Jibbon Head, Royal National Park, near Bundeena. Public material records the Dharawal name as Djeeban / Djeebahn (“sand bars at low tide”). The most extensive engraving complex in the park that is signposted for visitors. Public interpretive record: whales (including overlapping orcas), stingray, turtle, wallaby/kangaroo, and a human/ancestral figure.

A viewing platform and boardwalk keep feet off the rock. NSW National Parks and Wildlife Service records conservation work with Local Aboriginal Land Council and community. That is care, not a tour product of this sitting. We do not publish a site map. We do not use Jibbon as a Catch or Starline plate.

6. What this sitting does with this

  • Puts Country in the body of industrial briefs (Document 16), not a footnote.

  • Refuses to inherit a pad or name a berth over this record.
  • Leaves Songlines with custodians. Starlines stays this project’s own word.

We stand on Country that is already spoken for. Public teaching may be cited. It may not be worn.

Gold is the grid. The dark is still Country.
