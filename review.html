    02 · Geographic brief — surveyed facts only

    Two pages. No narrative overlay. No site is selected. Belts are labelled on every claim. Full source: GEO-BRIEF-ENGINEER.md.

    belt: science

    Port and industry already exist

    Port Hedland is a working deep-water bulk port on the Pilbara coast. Heavy industry, rail, and a FIFO construction workforce are already there. That is not the same thing as a recovery architecture. Orientation of an existing industrial machine is the claim — not a greenfield city.

    belt: science

    Critical minerals, dated

    Australia was the world's largest lithium producer in 2024 at roughly 36% of global mine output (Chile ~22%). The Pilbara also hosts iron ore at export scale and rare-earth feedstock (Mt Weld → Lynas). Minerals are a surveyed stock. Propellant and mid-stream processing are not automatic consequences.

    belt: science

    Legal corridor is in force

    The U.S.–Australia Technology Safeguards Agreement was signed 26 October 2023 and entered into force 23 July 2024. It is the treaty-level instrument for U.S. launch vehicles and related technology on Australian soil. AUKUS Pillar 2 sits alongside. The corridor exists. Access to it by an independent Australian entity is not evidenced.

    belt: science

    Re-entry on Australian soil has been done

    Varda's W-2 capsule — launched on a SpaceX rideshare — landed at the Koonibba Test Range, South Australia, on 28 February 2025: the first commercial spacecraft recovered on Australian soil, with further landings since. Approvals, range operations, and recovery logistics have been exercised. That is a range fact, not a Port Hedland berth.

    belt: science

    Dual-use sensing exists nearby

    Space-domain sensing on the north-west coast (including the AUKUS-related radar at Exmouth) supports tracking and re-entry awareness. Earlier drafts placed DARC in the Pilbara; the correction is Exmouth. Sensing is not a crane that can lift a 52-metre stage.

    belt: science

    Water is a constraint, not a backdrop

    The Great Artesian Basin covers ~1.7 million km²; recharge is slow and localised. The Lake Eyre Basin covers ~1.2 million km² and is highly episodic. Any water-dependent pathway (process water, testbed, feedstock) must be engineered against these figures, not around them.

    belt: docs-gov

    Free, Prior and Informed Consent is a hard door

    No site selection, survey, construction, or testbed work proceeds over an identified sacred site or songline path without FPIC from the relevant Traditional Owners, in advance. Floor: Aboriginal Heritage Act 1972 (WA) and Native Title Act 1993 (Cth). Ceiling: higher than the legal minimum — Juukan Gorge (May 2020) is why. This brief names no specific sacred site.

    belt: docs-gov

    What this page does not claim

    It does not select a site. It does not assert that Port Hedland is the optimal southern recovery port. It does not claim TerAustralis Incognita can operate a recovery berth. Those lines are Vision, held in the longer proposal, and are not forwarded here as surveyed.
